Counts / % switch — six ways

Each shown in a mock chart header at real size, beside the download and gear it shares the row with. Click them; they all work. Widths in the table at the bottom are measured, not estimated.

A. Shipped today — segmented, 30px, two words

Legible and self-explaining, and it takes 185px of a header that also has to hold the download and the gear. On a narrow panel it is most of the row. This is the thing to beat.

Breaks the 24px pill size, and the words are longer than the values they stand for.

B. Pill pair, 24px — the house primitive, at its documented size

.blockr-pill is defined for exactly this: a toggle whose label is the value, like asc/desc. Shortening the words to N and % is the whole saving; "Count distinct" was naming the mechanism, not the thing on the axis. Both options stay visible, so nobody has to click to find out what else there is.

Follows the rule and the size. My pick.

C. One square, click-through — shows the CURRENT mode  ·  CHOSEN

Your suggestion, smallest thing that can work. The catch is the oldest ambiguity in toggle design: a button reading % can mean "you are showing %" or "press for %", and users split roughly evenly on which. It also hides that a second mode exists at all.

Shipped 2026-08-28, at 30px so it matches the download and the gear exactly — three identical squares read as one control row. The tooltip carries both halves ("Showing % of panel — click for Count distinct"), which is the mitigation for the ambiguity below, not a cure for it.

Standing weakness: the label alone reads either way, and a second mode is not advertised.

D. One square, % as a mode — pressed or not, like Bold

Same footprint as C, none of the ambiguity: the button is always %, and its state says whether you are in it. Pressed means percent, not pressed means counts. Reads like the B / I buttons everyone already knows. The cost is that "counts" is never named, it is only the absence of %.

The compact answer, if 52px is still too much. Only works while there are exactly two modes.

E. Sliding two-up, 24px — iOS-style segmented

Same information as B in a slightly softer shell; the moving knob makes the switch obvious. Not a primitive the design system has, so it would be a new component to maintain for a cosmetic gain over B.

Nice, but a new primitive for no capability B lacks.

F. In the status footer — zero header cost

The footer already exists on every chart and already carries state ("No filter active", the label-thinning note). Putting the switch there costs no header space at all and lets the control say the whole sentence. Against it: the footer is where people look after reading the chart, not before, and it is the quietest surface on the card.

Free space-wise, but it is the quietest place on the card for a control a clinician uses constantly.

Recommendation: B. It is the primitive the design system already defines for a toggle whose label is the value, at the size it documents, and it keeps both options visible. Take D instead if the header is still too tight — same clarity, less than half the width, at the cost of never naming "counts". A is what ships today and should go either way.
